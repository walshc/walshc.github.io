Question 2

You are a mortgage broker and you use Python to help you give advice to your clients. Your clients often ask about the difference in payment streams between a linear and annuity mortgages. In short, in a linear mortgage you pay off the loan amount in equal installments and pay interest monthly on the remaining loan amount at the time, whereas under an annuity mortgage you pay the exact same amount each month. A linear mortgage costs less overall, but comes with higher payments at the start of the loan.

Formally, let L_0 be the loan amount (principal) which is issued in month 0. The borrower makes payments for T months starting in month 1, where T is the loan duration in years multiplied by twelve (to convert to months). The annual interest rate is r.

In a linear mortgage, the borrower pays off \frac{L_0}{T} of the principal each month t. The remaining loan amount at time t is therefore L_t=L_0 -\left(t-1\right)\frac{L_0}{T}. The borrower additionally pays interest on the remaining loan amount equal to \frac{r}{12}L_t. Therefore each period the borrower pays:1 M^{linear}_t=\frac{L_0}{T} + \frac{r}{12}L_t

In an annuity mortgage, the idea is to find a constant monthly payment M^{annuity} that will pay off the loan in exactly T months. This number can be solved analytically2 and is equal to:

M^{annuity}=\frac{\frac{r}{12} L_0 }{1-\left(1+\frac{r}{12}\right)^{-T}}

At the moment you use the following Python functions to do this, where the arguments in the first two are:

  1. amount - the size of the loan in euros.
  2. rate - the annual interest rate (3% should be expressed as 0.03).
  3. duration - the duration of the mortgage in years.

The argument payments in functions 3-5 is a list containing the monthly payments from months 1 to T.

def linear_mortgage_monthly_payments(amount, rate, duration):
    num_payments = duration * 12
    r = rate / 12
    principal_repayment = amount / num_payments
    remaining_amounts = [amount - (t - 1) * principal_repayment \
        for t in range(1, num_payments + 1)]
    interest_payments = [r * x for x in remaining_amounts]
    return [principal_repayment + x for x in interest_payments]

def annuity_mortgage_monthly_payments(amount, rate, duration):
    num_payments = duration * 12
    r = rate / 12
    m = r * amount / (1 - (1 + r)**(-num_payments))
    return [m for i in range(num_payments)]

def total_payments(payments):
    return sum(payments)

def first_payment(payments):
    return payments[0]

def last_payment(payments):
    return payments[-1]

For an example a 30-year mortgage of €300,000 at 4% interest, this gives:

linear = linear_mortgage_monthly_payments(300000, 0.04, 30)
annuity = annuity_mortgage_monthly_payments(300000, 0.04, 30)
print("Total payments under linear are", total_payments(linear))
print("Total payments under annuity are", total_payments(annuity))
print("First payment under linear is", first_payment(linear))
print("First payment under annuity is", first_payment(annuity))
print("Last payment under linear is", last_payment(linear))
print("Last payment under annuity is", last_payment(annuity))
